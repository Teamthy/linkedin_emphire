## REPLY (paste this)
That's the harder path, and the right one: a twin built from observed behavior instead of a described persona.

The question I'd want answered before trusting one: how do you know which parts it gets wrong? A twin at 85% fidelity is useful, but only if you know which 15% is the other 15. Otherwise you get confident results precisely where it diverges from the person it is modeling, and those are the decisions people act on.

The second thing, which becomes a product problem before a legal one: a twin is a model of a real person. Data age, consent to be modeled, and what happens when someone changes their mind all need answers, because "we built it from their behavior last year" stops being true the moment they change.

How are you scoring fidelity today?

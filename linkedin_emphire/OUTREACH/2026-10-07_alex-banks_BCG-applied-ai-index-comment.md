## COMMENT (paste this)
Point 2 is where the real story is, and BCG's own follow-up on the authorization gap goes further than the summary.

Every access control we have assumes human judgment. A support rep can see account histories and send email on company letterhead, and no rule stops them blasting the client list, because they never would. Judgment plus consequences did the work. Agents have neither.

Worse, the split of high-risk work across two people exists so nobody can complete it alone. Give one agent broad access and it enters the invoice and approves the payment itself. A check that held for decades is just gone.

And a control only counts if someone has the authority and the muscle memory to use it. An off switch nobody has pulled is a design, not a control.

So the 5% is measuring paperwork. The better question is how many of the 42% have watched their limits get pushed by something actively trying to exceed them.

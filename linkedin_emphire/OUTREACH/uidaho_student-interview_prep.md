# Student Interview Prep — University of Idaho (Kevin Chang)
Matched by: Kevin Chang (Director & Professor, UIdaho College of Engineering)
Format: 15-20 minutes, virtual (Zoom/Teams likely)
Audience: FIRST-YEAR engineering student (18-19 years old, early in the program, possibly undecided on specialization)

## WHAT IT IS (and what it ISN'T)
✅ IS: an informational / career chat. The student asks YOU questions about your work, your path, what engineering is actually like day-to-day.
✅ IS: the student's assignment. They will probably have a short list of required questions from Kevin (see below).
✅ IS: 15-20 minutes. SHORT. Don't over-prepare a presentation.
❌ IS NOT: a technical interview. Nobody will ask you to code or explain transformer architectures.
❌ IS NOT: a job interview (for you or for them).
❌ IS NOT: a keynote. Don't lecture. You're a real person they get to talk to, not a TED Talk.
❌ IS NOT: a recruiting pitch for AI or for Lagos or for your work. Keep it about them.

## LIKELY FLOW OF THE 15-20 MINUTES
(Student will usually lead; if they freeze, you gently carry it.)

Minute 0-2 — Greeting, quick warm-up
  - Smile, camera on, say hi, confirm you're Olusanya, software engineer in Lagos, thank them for reaching out
  - "So what made you pick engineering?" is an easy opener if they seem nervous

Minute 2-12 — Their questions (they'll usually have a list)
  - Common first-year questions are listed below. Answer honestly, short, human. No corporate answers.
  - They're 18 — "I don't know" or "that part was really hard for me" answers are more useful to them than polished success stories.

Minute 12-17 — You ask THEM a question or two back
  - "What made you choose engineering?"
  - "What's the thing you're most excited about so far? Most confused by?"
  - "Is there anything about real engineering that your classes aren't teaching yet that you're curious about?"
  This is where you go from "another practitioner" to "the engineer they remember."

Minute 17-20 — Wrap up
  - "Feel free to connect with me on LinkedIn if you ever have questions as you go through the program — I'm always happy to help a student."
  - Wish them well in their first semester, sign off warmly.

## QUESTIONS THEY WILL PROBABLY ASK (have short, human answers ready)
Kevin likely gives them a standard list like:
1. What do you do day-to-day as an engineer?
   Your answer should be HONEST. Most of your day is reading code, debugging, talking to people, writing tests, attending standups — not writing brilliant new features from scratch. First-years have a movie idea of engineering; you can demystify it.
2. How did you get into engineering?
   THIS IS YOUR SUPERPOWER. Med Lab Science → self-taught SE from Lagos → multi-domain builder. 99% of their other interviewees went straight from high school to engineering school to a local job. Your story is the one they'll remember. Don't over-dramatize, but tell it straight.
3. What skills or classes were most useful to you?
4. What do you wish you'd known as a first-year?
   Good answers: "how much of the job is communication not code," "that it's okay to not be the best in the room," "that building small things beats consuming tutorials," "that nobody has it figured out even 10 years in."
5. What advice would you give a first-year engineering student?
6. What's the hardest part of the job?
7. (Sometimes) What kind of projects should I be working on?

Answer each in 30-60 seconds. Stories beat advice. Instead of "don't be afraid to switch paths," say "I graduated in medical lab science and didn't write my first line of real software until I was 23, and it worked out."

## WHAT MAKES YOU MEMORABLE (in a good way)
- You are NOT another white/male/Boise/Seattle/Microsoft/Hewlett-Packard engineer. You are a Nigerian who switched from medical lab science, builds across web/AI/CV/e-commerce/DeFi from Lagos. That's the interview they tell their roommates about.
- You don't have to TRY to be interesting. Just being honest about your path is enough.
- If they ask "what's a cool thing you've worked on?" — you have sorghum lodging CV, e-commerce integrations, AI tools. Pick one small, visual, cool-sounding thing (the sorghum project is perfect) and explain it simply. No jargon.
- Be KIND. First-years are nervous. If they fumble a question, smile and help them out.
- Remember their name (they'll tell you at the start). Use it once.

## WHAT NOT TO DO
- ❌ Don't lecture them for 20 minutes about your career. Ask them questions too.
- ❌ Don't scare them ("oh you have no idea how hard it is out there") — they're 18. Encourage honestly, don't project burnout.
- ❌ Don't oversell AI as the only future. They're first-years who haven't picked a specialization yet. Let engineering be broad for them.
- ❌ Don't say "I wish I'd never become an engineer" or other cynical stuff. They're at the very start.
- ❌ Don't be late. Show up 2 minutes early, camera on, mic working.
- ❌ Don't talk down. They're a student but they're smart enough to detect condescension.
- ❌ Don't plug your LinkedIn, your posts, your services, anything. The point is to help a student.

## NICE EXTRA TOUCHES (small, high ROI)
- After the call, send them a LinkedIn connection request with a one-line note: "Great chatting with you [Name] — good luck with your first semester. Feel free to reach out if you ever have questions."
- When you email/thank Kevin afterwards, mention ONE specific thing the student said or asked ("[Student] asked me about non-traditional paths into engineering and it made me think...") — that's how Kevin knows you actually engaged, not just showed up.
- Keep a note of the student's name + what they were interested in; if you post the reflection post afterwards, you can reference the conversation generically ("spoke with a first-year engineer at UIdaho this week who asked me...") without naming them.

## IF THEY FREEZE / DON'T HAVE QUESTIONS
Have 2-3 gentle prompts ready:
- "Want me to tell you what a typical Tuesday looks like for me?"
- "What's made you curious about engineering so far?"
- "Is there any part of engineering that feels intimidating right now? I can tell you whether it stays scary."

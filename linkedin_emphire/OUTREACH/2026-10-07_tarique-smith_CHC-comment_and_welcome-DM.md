## COMMENT (paste this)
Six primitives is a clean cut, and deny-overrides-approval is the right default. The thing I would stress test is what the boundary gets checked against at execution time.

The failure I keep finding is not forged authority. It is real authority that quietly drifted. An agent gains a tool on a Tuesday. A service account gets widened for a debugging session and never narrowed back. Now every action is being rechecked against a scope that stopped describing reality weeks ago.

Same question for revocation mid-flight: if a tool call already fired before consent was pulled, what is the corrective action, and does it get recorded as a reversal or just as a new grant?

Evidence is the primitive I would lean hardest on. Signed records are only as useful as an operator's ability to read them, and most teams treat logging as detection when it is really a record of what already happened.

## COMMENT (paste this)
The Two Rooms Problem has a third room: production. The earnings call describes what AI can do. The team on the ground sees what it actually does, including what quietly fails and what a human is quietly cleaning up.

There is a cost in the headcount math that never reaches the slide. Much of what entry-level people did was verification: checking the output, catching the weird case, noticing the thing that looks fine and isn't. That work is also what keeps AI systems from embarrassing a company in public. Cut the bottom rungs and you do not only lose future judgment. You lose the people who currently catch what the model gets wrong.

Which makes your line about the person never hired land harder. The biggest AI layoff may also be the quiet loss of the last line of defense.

I am building my career inside this exact window, so I read this one twice.

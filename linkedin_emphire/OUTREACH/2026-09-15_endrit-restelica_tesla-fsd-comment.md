## TOP-LEVEL COMMENT (RECOMMENDED, ~1350 chars)

As someone who builds CV systems, I'm with Richard and Kai-Marcel on the hardware question — vision-only depth perception on a cliff edge in bad lighting is a real CV problem, not a hot take. LiDAR solves specific failures cameras fundamentally can't.

But the bigger blind spot is what Jai started pointing out: once human drivers figure out how autonomous cars behave, they exploit it.

If you know the car always yields, you pull out in front of it every time. If you know it reverses slowly on narrow roads, you cut it off. That's not a physics problem — it's an adversarial behavior problem. On a two-ton vehicle at 60km/h, that's real damage.

This is the same gap I see in every AI system. The demo works great when everyone cooperates. Production happens when bad actors, edge cases, and people who understand the rules start gaming them. Gustavo's point below about calibrated trust — systems that show their own limits before the hard moment, not after — is the part every team building AI still hasn't solved.

The mountain-road clip is the two-hour demo. The hard part is the next twenty-two hours of "what can go wrong when people, weather, and physics don't cooperate."

What scares me most isn't the car failing on a cliff on camera. It's the first teenager who figures out you can bully an FSD car out of a lane just by committing first.

Gustavo — this. Calibrated trust is the single most under-discussed problem in every AI system right now, and Parasuraman & Riley nailing this in 1997 makes it all the more frustrating that almost nobody shipping today seems to have read it.

Every AI I've shipped that failed did so not because it made a wild error but because it was quietly wrong in a way the user didn't see coming — 98% confidence on a hallucinated answer, smooth steering right up to the moment it doesn't see the motorbike.

This is where red teaming AI isn't just about breaking the model — it's about testing whether users understand what the system CAN'T do, in the moment they need to know it.

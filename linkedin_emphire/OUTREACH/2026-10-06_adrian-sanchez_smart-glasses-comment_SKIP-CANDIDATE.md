## COMMENT (only if used)
The capture is not the interesting part. The retention is.

A camera on someone's face is visible. What happens downstream is not: which model matches it, what gets stored, how long, who can query it, and what it gets sold into. Glasses are the front door. The database behind them is where the harm actually lives, and it is the part nobody can see or audit.

On the CV side, this is also measurable rather than theoretical. Face matching false-positive rates are well documented, and they are not evenly distributed across skin tones and age groups. The risk from these devices is not that they see you. It is that they confidently misidentify someone, and a decision gets made on that.

So the Norway question is the right one. But the rule should target the pipeline, not just the hardware.

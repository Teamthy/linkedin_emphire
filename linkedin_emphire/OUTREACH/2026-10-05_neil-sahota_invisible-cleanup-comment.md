## COMMENT (paste this)
The line that gets me is "buried that work under invisible human cleanup."

Three teams independently built verification layers, which means three teams independently found the same failures and wrote them down somewhere. Probably in spreadsheets nobody is reading.

That's the part most organizations miss: the fix was already in the building. Every hand-check is a labeled example of a failure. Capture those and you have a red team running itself every day, for free. Leave them in spreadsheets and you have expensive humans quietly absorbing the system's mistakes.

The productivity numbers never showed it because the failure never got a name. No incident, no post-mortem. Just people quietly rechecking fields.

Curious whether the 28% came from the reinforcement loops themselves, or from the corrections finally becoming visible to the system.

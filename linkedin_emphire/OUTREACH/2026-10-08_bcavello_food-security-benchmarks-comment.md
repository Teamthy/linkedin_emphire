## COMMENT (paste)
The work here matters, and one benchmark dimension I hope gets weight: the languages and channels farmers actually use, and the cost when the advice is wrong.

Most advisory AI is built and tested in English, on clean inputs. Smallholder advisory runs in Swahili, Hausa, Pidgin, often on low bandwidth, often as voice or SMS where confident-wrong and confident-right are indistinguishable. If the benchmark measures accuracy on curated Q and A, it certifies tools that fail quietly at the last mile.

Advice about seed and fertilizer does not get a retry button. A hallucinated answer costs a season, not a support ticket.

That is the failure surface I test for in other domains. Food security deserves the same rigor on day one.

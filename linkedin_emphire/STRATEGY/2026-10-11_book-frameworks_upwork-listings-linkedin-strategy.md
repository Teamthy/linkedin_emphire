## 1. Framework extraction (what we actually use)

### From the offer book (Hormozi, *$100M Offers*)
| Framework | What the book says | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Value Equation** | Four drivers of perceived value: dream outcome and perceived likelihood of achievement (raise these), and time delay and effort and sacrifice (cut these). Hormozi argues the time and effort levers are harder to copy, because anyone can make a big promise. (The formula diagram itself did not extract from the text file, so the wording above is from the prose.) | Used to rewrite each tier's custom description (section 2). |
| **Problems → Solutions → Trim & Stack** | List every problem the buyer has, turn each into a solution, then cut the high-cost/low-value items first and the low-cost/low-value items next. Keep low-cost/high-value and high-cost/high-value items. | Used to decide what goes in each tier and what becomes a paid add-on. |
| **Starving crowd** | Price follows how badly the market needs the outcome. | Used to pick which buyer segment to lead with (section 2). |
| **Guarantees** | The top objection is risk. Reversing risk is one of the fastest ways to raise conversion. Hormozi cites Jason Fladlien, who saw conversion on an offer move 2 to 4 times from guarantee quality alone. The math: a bigger guarantee usually raises net revenue even if refunds rise. Four types: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee, implied. | Candidate for the "re-test or next round free" guarantee in the plan. Needs your approval first (open decision 3). |
| **Naming** | A name determines how well ads, cold outreach and inbound comments convert. Generate many names so the offer does not fatigue. | Used for tier titles and the LinkedIn offer names. |
| **Scarcity and urgency** | Scarcity limits how many; urgency limits when. Use only real deadlines. | Use only real capacity limits and real delivery dates. No fake countdowns. |
| **Bonuses** | Add value that is cheap for you to give and high-perceived-value for the buyer. | Maps to the add-ons already in the catalog (section 2). |

### From the branding book (Hormozi, *How to Start, Grow, then Pivot Your Own Brand*)
| Framework | What the book says | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Bouquet metaphor** | A brand is the set of things you consistently associate with. Pick the flowers (topics, people, values) that match the audience you want. Remove the ones that do not fit. | LinkedIn stays on one bouquet: AI features breaking, hardening, and shipping. Off-topic content is removed. |
| **Five directions to move** | Up market, down market, broader, narrower, adjacent. Any move should be net positive: gain more audience than you lose. | Current bet: narrow to "AI features that ship with a tested failure log" (solo engineer). Move up-market only after reviews exist. |
| **Promises made and kept** | Brand comes from delivering what you promise, repeatedly. Bad delivery compounds into negative word of mouth. | Every catalog promise (for example, "four-part findings format every time") must be delivered exactly. |
| **Brand pivot is measured, not guessed** | Watch which audience reacts, and move back toward the people who engage with the work. | Weekly scoreboard (section 5). Reposition only on evidence. |

### From the lead book (Hormozi, *$100M Leads*)
| Framework | What the book says | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Core Four** | Four ways to let people know about what you sell: warm 1-to-1 (warm outreach), 1-to-many to a warm audience (posting content), 1-to-1 to a cold audience (cold outreach), 1-to-many to a cold audience (paid ads). | The core of section 4. |
| **Give:ask ratio** | Give far more than you ask. "Jab, jab, jab, right hook." The book cites roughly 3.5:1 for television and about 4:1 for Facebook feed ads. Under-ask on purpose. | Target at least 3 value posts per 1 offer post. |
| **Content unit: hook, retain, reward** | Every content piece must hook attention, keep it, and then reward it. | Structure for every LinkedIn post (section 4). |
| **Lead getters** | Other people's Core Four: customers who refer, employees, agencies, affiliates and partners. Leverage beats doing it yourself. | Referral asks to past clients and peers. Partner agencies for overflow (section 4). |
| **Open To Goal** | Commit to an outcome count, not a time block: work until the number is hit. | Used for the 5 proposals per day habit, reframed as "until the day's count is done". |

### From the sales book (Suby, *Sell Like Crazy*)
| Framework | What the book says | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Market pyramid** | About 3% of any market is ready to buy. About 17% are gathering information, about 20% are problem-aware, and about 60% are not thinking about it. Most sellers chase the 3%. The money is in the 37% in the middle. | Target the 37%: teams with an AI feature in review, or a recent incident. |
| **Dream buyer and hair-on-fire problem** | Name one dream buyer and their most persistent, urgent problem. Title the bait around that problem. | Each listing and LinkedIn series names one buyer and one problem (section 2). |
| **High-Value Content Offer (HVCO)** | A free asset that solves a real problem and earns the right to the sale. Its title drives the response. | Our HVCO is the sample findings report and the starter checklist (already in the repo). Upwork rules keep off-platform links out of the listing. |
| **Godfather offer** | An irresistible offer = what they want + what they must do to get it. Weak offers ("download our guide") fail. | Each tier states an exact deliverable, a time, and an explicit next step. |
| **Magic Lantern** | Turn the non-buying majority with 2 to 3 pieces of pure value, each ending with a call to action to book. | Adapted to text and PDF follow-ups (no video for galleries). See section 4. |
| **Sell like a doctor** | Diagnose before prescribing. Use a free, zero-pressure 30 to 60 minute conversation. | Standard and Advanced buyers get the free diagnostic call. The debrief is the "prescription". |

## 2. Mapping onto the two Upwork listings

Both listings use one listing with three built-in tiers. Pricing and copy below are taken from `UPWORK/project_catalog.md`. Recommendations are marked **(rec)**.

### Listing 1: AI red team and prompt-injection testing
Dream buyer: a product or ops lead whose AI feature is in review, or who has had a recent incident. Hair-on-fire problem: "I have a chatbot or agent customers can break, and I do not know how badly."

| Tier | Value Equation read | Trim & Stack read | Recommendation (rec) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter: AI Quick Scan** (3 days, $150 / $200 / $300) | Time delay is already the strongest lever (3 days). Likelihood is shown by the sample findings report. Effort is low: a staging link and 3 answers. | Cheap to deliver, high perceived value as a first look. Keep it as the entry point. | Lead Upwork proposals with "3-day answer" wording (time delay is the top lever in the Value Equation). |
| **Standard: AI Red Team Review** (7 days, $600 / $800 / $1,200) | Dream outcome: "ship without a known hole". Likelihood: the 4-layer test plus a live debrief. Effort: no code changes, so the client's team is free. | The 20+ vector test and debrief are high value and cheap to deliver. Keep both. | Mention the debrief in the first paragraph. It is the "sell like a doctor" step. |
| **Advanced: Pre-Launch Hardening** (14 days, $1,500 / $2,000 / $3,000) | Dream outcome: "fixes stay fixed". Likelihood: a regression suite the client owns. Effort: the heaviest (repo access, a technical contact). | Code fixes are expensive to deliver, so cap their scope in the proposal. The 2-week support window is a cost, so keep it at this tier only. | Pre-scope with a repo checklist before you accept. Do not let scope grow without a change order. |

**Add-ons, read through Trim & Stack:**
- Executive summary (+$100): high perceived value for leaders, very cheap to deliver. Keep it. Best upsell for the Standard tier when the buyer is non-technical.
- Extra 30-minute debrief (+$75): cheap, high value. Keep it.
- Rush delivery (+$75): costs you schedule. Keep it paid.

**Pricing guardrail (from research, section 3):** the testing category is growing. QA testing rose 20% month over month in June 2026 (Upwork). Do not discount Quick Scan below the catalog's $150 floor to win the first review. Price the first review, not the margin.

### Listing 2: AI chatbot, agent, and full-stack build
Dream buyer: a business owner or product team that wants a working AI feature with no security surprises. Hair-on-fire problem: "I need a chatbot or agent live, and I cannot afford a demo that breaks in front of customers."

| Tier | Value Equation read | Trim & Stack read | Recommendation (rec) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter: Chatbot MVP** (5 days, $300 / $400 / $600) | Time delay (5 days) is the lever. Effort is low: describe the bot and give access. | Simple FAQ or support bot. Cheap and fast to deliver. | Price Small at $300, which is the floor of the benchmark band for a simple FAQ bot (section 3). |
| **Standard: RAG / Agent Build** (10 days, $900 / $1,200 / $1,800) | Likelihood: named integrations and a fixed scope. Effort: the client gives data access. | Tool/API integration and bug fixes are real engineering cost. Keep them and price them accordingly. | Put the data-access requirement first in the proposal so scope surprises stay out of the project. |
| **Advanced: Full-Stack AI Feature** (21 days, $2,500 / $3,500 / $5,000) | Likelihood: the red team pass before handoff. Dream outcome: "a shippable feature I own". Effort: client shares stack and hosting preferences. | Full-stack work plus the red team pass is the highest cost to deliver. Keep the red team pass included because it is the differentiator. | Lead with the red team pass. It is the reason to choose this tier over a cheaper developer. |

**Add-ons:** extra integration (+$150), rush delivery (+$200), 30 days of post-launch support (+$250). All three are high-cost work for you. Keep them as paid add-ons, never included.

**The link between the two listings (cross-sell):** Listing 2 Advanced includes a red team pass, and Listing 1 is the standalone version of that same pass. That is the upsell path: a buyer who comes in for a Quick Scan on an existing bot can move to Listing 2 for a rebuild.

**Open pricing decision (see section 7):** Listing 2 pricing still says "pending confirmation" in the catalog. Recommendation: match Listing 1's company-size structure (Small / Medium / Large), so both listings are priced the same way and the proposal copy stays consistent. Confirm on the live Upwork pricing screen before publishing.

## 3. Upwork research feeding the strategy

Full sources, dates and confidence levels are in `UPWORK/RESEARCH_TRACKER.md`. The findings that change the plan:

1. **Chatbot development demand is up 71% year over year** (Upwork, In-Demand Skills 2026). Supports keeping Listing 2 as a second listing, not folding it into Listing 1.
2. **AI-remediation work is growing fast.** Jobs asking humans to fix, refine or finish AI-generated work are up 70% year over year, and 8.3x in software since 2023 (Upwork, August 2026). This is the buyer for Advanced tiers on both listings: "the AI draft exists, now make it production-ready."
3. **Generic AI execution is commoditising; complex AI-augmented work is not.** Per Upwork's Future Workforce Index 2026, generic gen-AI contract volume grew 90% while per-contract pay fell 13%, and complex AI-augmented projects saw earnings rise 22% to 45%. Practical rule: never position a tier as "I run ChatGPT for you". Position each tier as judgment and testing on top of the model.
4. **Upwork's own chatbot pricing bands** (upwork.com/hire/ai-chatbot-specialists): rule-based FAQ or support bots about $300 to $1,200 per project; conversational NLP bots about $1,000 to $3,000; custom LLM or RAG assistants about $1,500 to $5,000; multichannel integration about $1,200 to $4,500. Listing 2 targets sit inside these bands. Note the page's date is not clear from the fetch.
5. **Search language is shifting to roles.** Secondary coverage of Upwork's July 2026 data says "AI developer" and "AI engineer" are now among the top searched AI terms, and "AI integration" and "full stack AI developer" are among the fastest rising. See open decision 4 on the Listing 2 search tags.

## 4. LinkedIn content strategy, mapped to the Core Four

**Positioning (bouquet):** "I build AI features and I break them before your users do." Every post belongs to that bouquet. Remove posts about anything else, even if they get views. The branding book's warning applies: off-topic views are the wrong views.

**Dream buyer for content:** the 37% (teams with an AI feature in review or a recent incident). Ignore the 3% who are already on Upwork looking for a Quick Scan. Those buyers are reached through Upwork, not LinkedIn.

### The Core Four, applied

| Core Four channel | Book mechanic | Our application | Weekly target (from plan) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm outreach (1-to-1, warm)** | Ask your network personally. Lead getters start here. | Message 10 likely referrers (past clients, former colleagues, peers). Ask one specific question each, then send the free test link. No public mention of the Sudhir engagement. | 3 warm messages/week |
| **Post free content (1-to-many, warm)** | Give more than you ask. Use the content unit (hook, retain, reward). | 2 posts/week per the plan. For each offer post, publish 3 value posts. Use the hook bank in `EMPIRE/content_bank.txt`. Every post ends with one soft call to action, not an ask for a sale. | 2 posts/week |
| **Cold outreach (1-to-1, cold)** | Trust is the barrier. Warm up with engagement before you message. | Comment on buyer posts first (5/day per plan). Only then send a short DM, and only to people whose post shows the problem. Each DM names their problem, not your service. | 5 comments/day, DMs after engagement |
| **Paid ads (1-to-many, cold)** | Scale only a proven offer. | Deferred. Per the plan, only after $1,000 is booked, capped at $100 for 30 days. Ads are for a proven HVCO, not for an untested offer. | None until Phase 3 |

**Lead getters, applied:** ask past clients for one referral by name. Ask agencies and freelance peers who build AI features but do not test them, and offer a partner rate (rec: a referral fee or a shared delivery arrangement, to be decided with you). Do not hand out your own pricing as a partner rate without a written agreement.

### Content unit for each post (hook, retain, reward)
- **Hook:** one specific, bold line about how AI features fail. Example pattern: "An AI chatbot leaked its system prompt in attempt 2 of 6."
- **Retain:** the specific break, with the exact prompt type and what happened (from the repo's 6-attempt log).
- **Reward:** the fix or the takeaway, plus one quotable line (analytics in the plan says nothing gets reposted without one).

### Magic Lantern for non-buyers (adapted, not video)
Upwork galleries are images only. For LinkedIn and email, use text and PDF follow-ups instead of video:
1. Follow-up 1 (value): the one-page starter checklist, as a PDF.
2. Follow-up 2 (value): one finding from the sample findings report, with the fix.
3. Follow-up 3 (offer): "If your feature is in review this month, here is the free 30-minute test. Book a slot here."

Video is optional outside Upwork galleries if you decide to record a screen capture later. The catalog already flags this as a future option, and no video is planned now.

### Eight post angles mapped to the frameworks

| # | Angle | Framework | Post type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "The first thing I try on any new chatbot" | Hook / retain / reward | Value |
| 2 | One real break from the 6-attempt log, with the fix | Content unit | Value (case note) |
| 3 | "A prompt without a threat model is a demo, not a product" | Bouquet (narrow) | Value |
| 4 | Why the Value Equation's "time delay" matters for AI pilots | Value Equation | Value |
| 5 | "Your AI draft needs a human pass before launch" (AI-remediation data) | Upwork research, remediation demand | Value (research-backed) |
| 6 | The four layers an AI red team must cover | Dream buyer | Value (HVCO teaser) |
| 7 | Free 30-minute test: what it covers and who it is for | Godfather offer, sell like a doctor | Offer |
| 8 | Case note on a delivered finding (only if already delivered and cleared to share) | Proof, give:ask | Value or offer |

Use roughly 3 value posts (angles 1 to 6 and 8) to 1 offer post (7), in that order over each week.

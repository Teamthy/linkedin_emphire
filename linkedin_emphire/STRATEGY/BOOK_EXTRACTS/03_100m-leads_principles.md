## 1. Principles in the book

| # | Principle | What the book says (short quotes kept exact) |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Measure engaged leads, not leads** | A lead is someone who gave contact details. An engaged lead has signalled real interest. Track the engaged-lead count. |
| L2 | **The Core Four: four ways to reach people** | Only four: warm outreach (1-to-1 to warm audience), posting content (1-to-many to warm), cold outreach (1-to-1 to cold), paid ads (1-to-many to cold). "If you aren't getting as many leads as you want, you're not doing the core four with enough skill or with enough volume." |
| L3 | **Everyone has a list** | Phone contacts, email contacts, and social connections add up to a starting list, "for many of you, this will be your first 1000 leads." |
| L4 | **Warm outreach: ask for introductions before selling** | Contact people who know you. Use the A-C-A reply: Acknowledge what they said, Compliment a positive trait, Ask a follow-up that leads toward your offer. |
| L5 | **Contact up to three times, then stop** | "Once per day for three days or until they respond. Whichever comes first." Physical mail is once per week. |
| L6 | **Make the "do you know anybody" offer** | Ask who they know with a specific struggle, without asking them to buy. The book's pattern: describe the person you help (struggle, dream outcome, time), ask for an introduction, and let the listener connect the dots. |
| L7 | **Value elements in the pitch** | Dream outcome, perceived likelihood of achievement (proof, reviews, guarantees), time delay, effort and sacrifice. Maximize the first two and minimize the last two. (Same framework as the offer book.) |
| L8 | **Post in public, ask in private** | "When you use this strategy, you give in public, ask in private." |
| L9 | **Give until they ask** | Over-deliver free value so the audience asks for more. The book argues this produces the best customers, the biggest givers, and the most durable growth. |
| L10 | **Give:ask ratio** | Growing platforms give far more than they ask. Example from the book: a one-hour podcast with three 30-second ads is about 58.5 minutes of giving to 1.5 minutes of asking. Asking too often can shrink an audience. |
| L11 | **Two ways to ask: integrated and intermittent** | Integrated: a call to action inside every piece, placed after the valuable moment or at the end. Intermittent: periodic dedicated offers. Keep the give:ask ratio high either way. |
| L12 | **Cold outreach: personalize, then give big fast value** | Strangers do not know or trust you. Personalize with one to three real facts about them. Then give value they can feel in under thirty seconds. |
| L13 | **Lead magnet quality decides cold response** | The book's example: a "game planning session" (a sales call in disguise) performed poorly. Swapping to free real service roughly tripled the take rate. A weak lead magnet is ignored; a strong one stands out. |
| L14 | **Cold outreach is a numbers game** | Build the list first (software, brokers, manual research), then find the message, then increase volume. |
| L15 | **Paid ads: efficiency beats creativity** | "All advertising works. The only thing that differs between advertisements is how well they work." Good enough to scale is good enough. |
| L16 | **Three phases of ad spend** | Track money first. Accept planned losses while testing ("lose money"). Scale only proven ads ("print money"). |
| L17 | **Measure LTGP to CAC** | Lifetime gross profit (lifetime spend minus delivery cost) divided by customer acquisition cost. Above 1:1 is profitable; the book's observed threshold for scaling businesses is about 3:1. |
| L18 | **Benchmark CAC against industry averages** | If CAC is below 3 times the industry average, improve the business model (LTGP). If CAC is above 3 times, improve the ads (CAC). |
| L19 | **Client-financed acquisition** | If the customer's gross profit in the first 30 days covers the cost to acquire them, ad spend funds itself and cash is not the bottleneck. |
| L20 | **Customer referrals come from a product that delights** | "The best advertising is a happy customer. An amazing product turns every customer into a lead getter." Referrals have higher lifetime value and lower acquisition cost. |
| L21 | **Agencies: pay for time, insist on learning** | The book's example: pay for hourly time with a condition that you watch and learn, so you can take the work back in-house. |
| L22 | **Affiliates and partners take time to pay off** | The book's affiliate launch produced little in week one and then large sales from week four, driven by a launch-ready program and sustained follow-through. Expect a slow start. |
| L23 | **Leverage: other people's Core Four** | Customers, employees, agencies, and affiliates doing the core four for you is the leverage that scales. |
| L24 | **Open To Goal, and Rule of 100** | Rule of 100: 100 primary actions per day for 100 days. Open To Goal: work until a set number of outcomes is hit. Choose one and commit. |
| L25 | **Seven levels of advertisers** | Level 1: warm outreach. Level 2: consistent personal inputs plus content. Level 3: employees. Level 4: consistent referrals (about 25% or more of customers from referrals). Level 5: multiple methods across multiple platforms. |
| L26 | **Fix the product before scaling ads at level 4** | "This is where most people mess up. They let their product slip and never recover." |

## 2. Applicable to us

### A. Warm outreach (current constraint: no paid channel yet)
1. **Start with the list you already have (L3, L4).** Build the warm list from phone contacts, email contacts, past clients, former colleagues, and peers. Record each name in the contact file, with one personal reason to reach out.
2. **Use the A-C-A reply on every warm message (L4).** Acknowledge their post or situation, compliment one specific thing, then ask one question that leads to the problem space ("are you shipping an AI feature this quarter?").
3. **Three touches maximum, then stop (L5).** Per the book: one touch per day for three days, or until they respond. This matches the plan's 3 warm messages a week, provided each contact is touched at most three times.
4. **Use the "do you know anybody" ask for referrals (L6).** Name the buyer you serve (an ops or product lead with an AI feature in review), their struggle, and the result, and ask who they know. Do not ask them to buy. Ask for introductions. Each yes becomes a warm lead.
5. **Caution on the book's "free case studies" script.** The book's sample says "I'm taking on five case studies for free". Use that line only if the free work is actually being offered and is delivered. Do not use it as a rhetorical device. This follows the no-fabrication rule.

### B. Posting content (Core Four #2)
6. **Adopt "give until they ask" as the default (L8, L9).** The plan calls for 2 posts a week. Apply the give:ask ratio from L10: for every offer post, publish at least three value posts (the strategy file already sets this target).
7. **Give in public, ask in private (L8).** Public posts carry value and one soft call to action. Sales asks go to direct messages after someone engages. This resolves the tension between the plan's posting and outreach targets.
8. **Place calls to action after the valuable moment (L11).** Integrated CTAs go at the end of a post, after the lesson. Do not put an offer in the first two lines. Intermittent offers (a dedicated "free test" post) happen once every few weeks, not weekly.
9. **Lead magnet is the free test and the findings report (L13).** Test whether the lead magnet is "big fast value". The sample findings report is the strongest candidate. A generic "book a call" magnet will underperform, as the book's game-planning example shows.

### C. Cold outreach (Core Four #3, starts only after warm outreach is working)
10. **Build a targeted list by hand and within platform rules (L12, L14).** Use the book's list-building ideas (groups, communities, directories) but do not use scraping software to extract LinkedIn contact data. That breaks LinkedIn's user agreement. Manual research from public profiles is the approved route. Keep to the limits in `STRATEGY/2026-10-03_linkedin-free-limits_and-note-allocation.md`.
11. **Personalize with one to three real facts (L12).** Before any cold DM, note the person's recent post, role, or company problem. Open on that. Batch the research to keep it fast.
12. **Give big fast value in the first message (L12, L13).** The first message should carry something useful on its own: a finding, a one-page checklist, or a checklist of failure modes for their stack. Not "can we talk?".
13. **Measure the lead magnet, not just the message (L13).** If replies stay flat, change the magnet before the wording. The book's experience says the magnet is the bigger lever.

### D. Upwork as a lead source
14. **Upwork is a lead channel like the others and needs the same give-first discipline (L9, L11).** Proposal openers should give a specific insight about the buyer's failure mode before any pitch. Proposal templates in `UPWORK/proposal_templates.txt` should be reviewed against this rule.
15. **Keep off-platform asks out of Upwork (L8).** Upwork's rule against contact details on Upwork assets means the "ask in private" step is handled inside Upwork messaging.

### E. Paid ads: the gate for this plan
16. **Track before spending (L16).** The plan's $100 ad test is valid only after conversion tracking works end to end. Write the tracking set-up as the first ad task.
17. **Use LTGP to CAC as the gate (L17).** Before the test, compute: lifetime gross profit per client (fee minus delivery cost, times expected repeat work) divided by the cost to acquire one client. Target above 1:1 to break even and about 3:1 to scale. The plan's earlier wording ("margin times close rate") should use this ratio.
18. **Check the 3x industry benchmark (L18).** Look up typical acquisition costs for freelance security and AI consulting services. If our cost to acquire a client is above three times that average, the problem is the ads (targeting, creative). If it is below, the problem is the offer's value (LTGP).
19. **Test client-financed acquisition (L19).** Ask whether a Quick Scan's gross profit alone covers the cost of the ad that found the buyer, inside 30 days. If it does, ads can scale without cash strain. If not, a Standard tier or an add-on is needed to shorten payback.

### F. Referrals and reviews (Core Four leverage, Lead Getters #1)
20. **Earn referrals by delighting the buyer (L20).** For each Upwork job, the review ask is the minimum. The stronger move is an over-delivery the buyer repeats to peers: the debrief call, the executive summary, and the written repro for every failed test.
21. **Ask for the referral with the review (L20, L23).** The plan already asks for reviews within 24 hours. Add one line: "Who else in your network is shipping an AI feature?"
22. **Consider the level-4 threshold (L25, L26).** Once 25% of paid work comes from referrals, the business is ready for more capacity. Before that, improve delivery quality and review count. Do not add paid ads ahead of consistent referrals.

### G. Agencies and affiliates (later, not now)
23. **Agencies and partners: pay for time, insist on learning (L21).** When a partner agency offers to route work, ask for a paid working session that transfers the process, rather than a retainer that makes you dependent.
24. **Affiliate programs need a launch-ready package and patience (L22).** Not relevant until there are reviews and a repeatable Standard tier. Note this for the year-two plan.

### H. Cadence (Rule of 100 and Open To Goal, L24)
25. **Pick one and commit.** For LinkedIn, the safe version is Open To Goal on comments: engage until the day's count of meaningful comments is reached (the plan's 5 per day), not a 100-action rule. The 100-action rule would breach LinkedIn limits.

## 3. Gaps and cautions in this book
- The book is written for businesses that run paid ads at scale. Its ad benchmarks (3:1, 30-day payback) need our own cost data before they mean anything for us.
- Several key lessons are the author's own stories (gyms, supplements, affiliates). Use them as patterns, not as results we can expect.
- It assumes outbound list tools that conflict with LinkedIn's rules. Section 2C.10 is the adjustment.
- Freelance security testing has a far narrower buyer pool than a gym or a consumer product. Lead volume will be lower, so pipeline math should use deal value, not lead count.

I've rewritten this first post 11 times.

Every draft felt wrong — too braggy, too technical, too "here's my personal brand."
So I'm just going to tell you who I am.

My name is Olusanya Timothy. I'm from Lagos, Nigeria.
I studied Medical Laboratory Science. For years I trained to read samples and run diagnostics. I thought that was my track.

Then three things happened, in this order: curiosity, clarity, and purpose.

Curiosity got in the way first. I got curious about code — how software gets built, why some products feel alive and others feel dead on arrival. I did what every career switcher does first: I watched tutorials. A lot of them. Enough to feel productive without actually producing anything.

Then one day I closed the tutorial tab and tried to build something.
It broke. I fixed it. It broke again. I fixed it again.
That loop — broken, fixed, broken, fixed — is where the clarity came. I realized I didn't just want to study software. I wanted to ship it.

The purpose came last, and it was the quietest of the three. I wanted to build things that solve real problems for people like me — on this continent, building for this continent, and for the world beyond it.

Three years later, I'm a Software Engineer building across multiple domains:
→ Web applications with React, Next.js, Node.js, Go
→ AI integrations and LLM-powered products
→ Computer vision systems (including agricultural AI research)
→ Open source contributions
→ Work across edtech, proptech, fintech, e-commerce, and more

Some people will tell you to specialize early. Pick one lane and stay there.
That's probably good advice for some people. It hasn't been my path.
I go breadth first, depth when a problem demands it.

Here's what you can expect on this page:
→ Real lessons from shipping real things — no guru energy
→ Honest takes on AI — what works, what doesn't, when you shouldn't use it
→ The messy parts of being a self-taught engineer from Lagos building for global clients
→ Frameworks, not fluff — if something helped me, I'll break it down so you can steal it
→ Occasional posts on career, discipline, and the roads nobody tells you about

I'm not here to sell you a course. I'm not here to pretend I've arrived.
I'm here to build in public, think out loud, and connect with people building real things.

If that sounds like your corner of LinkedIn, stick around.
The best stuff is ahead of us, not behind.

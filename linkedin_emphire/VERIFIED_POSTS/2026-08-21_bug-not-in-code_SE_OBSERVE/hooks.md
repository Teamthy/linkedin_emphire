# HOOKS — Post #2 (Bug That Wasn't in My Code)

## 5 Hook Options

### Hook A — Story/curiosity gap (RECOMMENDED)
> "I spent three and a half hours today debugging a bug that wasn't even in my code."
- Type: Story / curiosity gap
- Predicted emotion: Recognition + curiosity
- Why recommended: Opens mid-story with a specific timeframe, no preamble, forces stop-scroll. Every engineer has been here and wants to know where the bug actually was.

### Hook B — Bold claim
> "The most expensive bugs I've ever fixed weren't logic errors. They were assumption errors."
- Type: Bold claim
- Predicted emotion: Nodding agreement from senior engineers

### Hook C — Humor/relatability
> "If debugging is the art of removing bugs, then programming must be the art of putting them in. Today I proved both."
- Type: Humor / quote twist
- Predicted emotion: Laugh + recognition

### Hook D — Question to audience
> "When was the last time you lost hours to a bug that turned out to be one stupid character?"
- Type: Direct question
- Predicted emotion: Instant recall — people want to comment their war story

### Hook E — Quiet truth
> "Real talk — I don't think you're a real engineer until you've blamed your code for a problem that was somewhere else entirely."
- Type: Identity / belonging
- Predicted emotion: Belonging + self-deprecating humor

---

**Selected:** Hook A (story/curiosity gap)
**Reason:** Strongest stop-scroll for a Friday audience; pairs the specific detail ("three and a half hours") with a universal engineer experience. Sets up the payoff cleanly.

Let me save you three months of tutorials.

You won't learn AI from courses.

Everyone sells one. Every other post is "learn AI in 30 days" or "the roadmap to becoming an AI engineer in 2026."

Courses teach you the terms. They'll show you what RAG is, what a transformer does, how to write a prompt chain. They will not teach you AI.

Here's the thing.

You learn AI the same way you learned every other part of software engineering. Build something. Break it. Clean up.

You learn prompt injection when a user slips one past your chatbot and it leaks PII — not when a tutorial defines it in bullet three.

You learn bias when your hiring model quietly penalizes non-Western names and you have to explain why to a client — not when a blog post warns you about it.

You learn red teaming when you try to break YOUR OWN system and can't (because you built it blind to the holes), then someone shows you three ways in on a Tuesday morning.

Courses teach the happy path. Production teaches everything else.

One rule I build by: if you haven't shipped something that broke on a real user, you haven't learned it yet. You've just read about it.

PS — What taught you more: the course, or the production bug that kept you up until 3am?

Real talk — I have a rule now.

No code merges after 4 PM on a Friday.

Not because I don't ship fast. Because I've been on every side of that call:
→ Merged a "harmless" refactor at 5:47 PM and debugged it Saturday morning
→ Got paged at 9 PM because a teammate did
→ Approved "one tiny change" on the way out the door and regretted it Monday

Here's the thing nobody really teaches you: the code at 10 AM Monday and the change at 5 PM Friday are not the same change.

It's not that you're suddenly a worse engineer. It's that the environment changes.
→ Reviewers skim when they're trying to leave
→ Testing gets lighter because QA is packing up
→ On-call coverage is thinner over the weekend
→ You're tired enough to dismiss the edge case you "know" won't happen
→ When something breaks, you're debugging alone with nobody to rubber-duck

People talk about code quality like it's a skill problem. A lot of the time it's a calendar problem.

One rule I ship by: if it can wait until Monday, it waits until Monday. The urgency is almost never real. The weekend you save will be your own.

PS — Engineers, am I the only one with a hard Friday cutoff? What's your rule?

Here's the part of the career-switch story nobody posts about: the 6 months where you're not a beginner anymore, but you're definitely not an engineer yet either.

You've finished the courses. You can explain what an API is. You've built a to-do app, a weather app, a blog — the things every tutorial tells you to build.

But you still don't feel like the real thing.

Job postings ask for 3 years experience. Real engineers on Twitter talk like they were born writing distributed systems. And you — ex-accountant, ex-teacher, ex-med-lab-scientist, ex-marketer — sit there wondering if you're fooling yourself.

I've been there. Here's what nobody tells you:

→ That in-between phase isn't a sign you're behind. It's the actual work.
→ The tutorials prepare you for nothing except more tutorials. The learning starts when you try to build something that breaks for reasons the course never covered.
→ Your old career isn't baggage. The med-lab training made me obsessive about edge cases and process. The teacher thinks differently about documentation. The accountant reads requirements differently. That's your edge, not your scar.
→ Nobody taps you on the shoulder and says "you're an engineer now." You start calling yourself one when you ship something that works, and you keep calling yourself one when the thing you shipped breaks and you fix it.

You don't cross a line one day. You just keep showing up until one day you look around and realize you've been doing the thing for a while.

If you're in that messy middle right now — keep going. You're closer than you feel.

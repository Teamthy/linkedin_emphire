# ARTICLE STRATEGY — REFRAMED FOR POSITIONING + AUDIENCE + LEADERSHIP + CLIENTS
Date: Fri Oct 2, 2026
Supersedes: earlier 21-articles plan where the tone was purely educational/peer-to-peer.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CORE THESIS SHIFT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
The series is NOT a free course. It is not a volunteer documentation project.
It is a strategic positioning asset that does four things simultaneously:

1. **POSITIONING**
   - Establishes you as THE go-to "software engineer who breaks AI before it breaks users"
   - Differentiates you from: (a) AI hype bros, (b) pure security consultants who don't build,
     (c) generic AI integration freelancers, (d) big-consulting AI risk teams
   - Geographic differentiator: Lagos-based, building for Global South realities
     (non-English inputs, tighter budgets, weaker grids, different failure surfaces)
   - Your positioning statement after 21 articles:
     "I'm the software engineer companies hire when they've shipped an AI feature that
     works in demos but breaks in production — and they want someone to find the holes
     before their users do."

2. **AUDIENCE BUILDING**
   - Each article must be shareable by three audiences:
     (a) Fellow engineers (they share the checklists)
     (b) Engineering leaders / CTOs (they share the "before you ship" framing)
     (c) Non-technical founders / product leaders (they share the "this is what you're
     missing, and it's not the demo" framing)
   - Every article ends with a clear, low-friction follow (comments + newsletter / DM CTA)
   - Tag people and companies where natural (Tarique, Simon Willison, the products you test)
   - Each article must have ONE quotable one-liner people repost as a standalone image/quote

3. **THOUGHT LEADERSHIP**
   - Each article introduces ONE new frame or term you can own
   - Counter-consensus takes, not "AI is important" content. Examples:
     * "The prompt is not a wall"
     * "Red teaming is not a security job. It's an engineering job."
     * "The demo is not the product" (you already have this — it's yours, own it)
     * "The work doesn't disappear. It shifts from fetching to verifying." (from Allie comment)
   - Every article must make a prediction or recommendation that readers can test against reality
   - Reference your own real projects and failures by name where possible (anonymize clients)

4. **CLIENT ACQUISITION (the quiet goal most engineers miss)**
   This is not "selling" in every post. It is engineering demand so that when a founder,
   CTO, or product leader reads enough of these articles, they DM you BEFORE they ship,
   not after the incident.

   How each article drives client acquisition without you pitching:
   - Demonstrates a repeatable methodology ("I do X before I ship")
   - Shows proof of work (you broke real systems, not theorized)
   - Names the EXACT PAIN a client has ("you shipped an AI feature that works in demos
     but breaks in production, and your team didn't catch it")
   - Ends with a SOFT, non-pushy CTA that invites inbound
   - Has enough case-study detail that the reader thinks "I need this person to look at our system"
   - Positions you as a doer, not a pundit. Clients hire doers.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
THE FOUR AUDIENCES YOU WRITE FOR EVERY TIME
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Every article must pass the "who forwards this?" test for at least one of these:

A. **Engineering leaders at AI-forward companies (hiring managers / CTOs at mid-stage startups
   to big tech doing AI integration)**: They read it and think "we need someone like this
   on our team" or "we should hire him to look at our system."

B. **Founders who shipped an AI feature and are quietly scared**: They read it and DM
   you saying "this is exactly what's happening with our product, can you help?"

C. **Fellow AI/software engineers (your peers)**: They read it, comment with their own
   war stories, repost to their teams, and amplify you. They are your distribution.
   They also become referrals when their boss asks "who actually understands this stuff?"

D. **AI security / red team community**: They read it and cite you, which brings
   credibility, invites to panels/podcasts, and the network that leads to bigger
   opportunities long-term.

ARTICLE STRUCTURE ADJUSTMENTS FOR THESE FOUR GOALS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
OLD STRUCTURE (educational, peer-to-peer):
1. War story
2. What the vulnerability is
3. How I tested it
4. What fixes it
5. 5-point checklist
6. PS question

NEW STRUCTURE (positioning + leadership + clients + audience):
1. **THE LEAD (1-2 paragraphs)** — Name the counter-consensus claim. State something
   most people believe that is wrong. Hook must be shareable on its own.
   Example: "Most AI security failures are not security problems. They're engineering
   problems, and the team that built the system is the last one that will find them."

2. **THE WAR STORY (1-2 paragraphs)** — Real, specific, personal. Names the cost of
   not doing this work. Client-friendly (anonymize). Shows your credibility without
   humblebragging. The reader should see themselves in the story.

3. **THE IDEA (2-3 sections)** — Explain the frame in plain English. Introduce ONE
   new term or framework that becomes yours (e.g. "the confirmation trap," "the demo
   vs. the product," "the happy path lie"). This is the thought-leadership move.

4. **THE PROOF (1-2 sections)** — Show your tests. Exact prompts. What broke. What
   didn't. Numbers where you have them. This is proof-of-work for clients. Specificity
   is what makes people hire you.

5. **THE RECOMMENDATION (1 section)** — What to do Monday morning. Not "hire me."
   Actionable steps a team can run on their own. Paradox: giving away the recipe
   makes people want to hire the chef.

6. **THE CHECKLIST (boxed/bulleted)** — 5-point steal-this checklist. Peers share
   this. Leaders print this. Clients use it to benchmark their own team and realize
   they have gaps (which drives them back to you).

7. **THE SOFT CTA (1 paragraph)** — NOT "buy my services." Something like:
   "If you're shipping an AI feature in the next quarter and you're not sure whether
   it survives contact with a real user, I help teams find the holes before their
   customers do. DM me or comment below — I reply to every note."
   Low pressure, clear offer, positioned around prevention (before the incident)
   not rescue (after it's too late).

8. **PS ENGAGEMENT QUESTION** — Same as before, drives comments.

POSITIONING LANGUAGE SHIFTS IN THE WRITING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
What changes in the prose itself:

DO say:
- "I help teams find these holes before they ship" (soft service signal)
- "The teams that win on AI will be the ones that..." (leadership framing)
- "Here's what I do before I ship any AI feature" (repeatable methodology)
- "If you're shipping an AI feature in the next quarter..." (speaking directly to buyers)
- Name yourself as having a framework ("my 30-minute starter test," "the seam between build and break")

DON'T say:
- "I'm just an engineer in Lagos" (overly self-effacing — say "I'm a software engineer in Lagos")
- "I'm not a security person" (this undermines positioning — say "you don't need to be a security person to do this work")
- Apologetic framing ("I'm new to this," "this is just my opinion") — you have proof of work, own it
- "You can do this yourself" in a way that eliminates your value — say "you can run the starter test yourself; if you want it done thoroughly, that's the work I do"

THE OFFER (what you actually sell, and how to say it quietly)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
At this stage you're not selling a course. You're not selling a product. You're selling:

1. **AI Red Teaming engagements for teams shipping AI features**
   Short, focused. 1-2 week engagements. Run red team tests on their AI system.
   Deliver a findings report + prioritized fix list. That is a clean, sellable offer.
   You don't need to name a price in the articles. Just make it clear you do this work.

2. **AI Integration consulting / retainer**
   For teams early in building an AI feature who want to build the guardrails in
   from day one, not bolt them on after an incident.

3. **Talks / workshops for engineering teams**
   Internal workshop on AI red teaming for engineers. 1-2 hours. Scalable offer.

How you signal this in articles without selling:
- Case studies ("a team I worked with recently found...")
- References to "engagements" or "teams I've helped"
- The CTA described above
- One line in your About/Featured that points to your work

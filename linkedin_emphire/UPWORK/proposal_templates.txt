PROPOSAL FORMULA + READY TEMPLATES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
Every proposal gets a custom first line. Nothing below is a
copy-paste script — it's a formula with examples. Log every
send in PROPOSALS/YYYY-MM-DD_client-or-jobtitle.md (mirrors
the OUTREACH/ convention on the LinkedIn side).

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
THE OPENER FORMULA (3 sentences, then stop)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. THEIR problem — name their exact product/feature and the
   one failure class most likely in it. Read the job post
   closely enough to be specific (chatbot? RAG? agent? which
   model/stack did they mention?).
2. YOUR receipts — the 6-attempts/4-breaks story, or a
   specific finding type relevant to their stack. One
   sentence, not the whole war story.
3. THE OFFER — point at the Quick Scan ($150) as the
   low-risk entry, or go straight to the Red Team Review if
   the job post signals budget/urgency.

Target length: 120-180 words. Longer reads as desperate.
Shorter reads as low-effort. No greeting line, no "I hope
this finds you well," no re-stating their whole job post back
to them.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
TEMPLATE A — chatbot / customer support AI job posts
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
```
If [their bot] handles refunds, account info, or anything a user could manipulate by rephrasing a request, it almost certainly has at least one prompt injection hole nobody's tested for yet. I ran six injection attempts against my own production chatbot last month. Four worked, including one that got it to promise a full refund to anyone who typed a single trigger word.

I'd run a focused test against [their bot] specifically: injection attempts, instruction-override attempts, and a check for whether it leaks its own system prompt. You'd get a findings report with exact repro steps and fixes, not just a pass/fail.

Happy to start with the $150 Quick Scan (15 tests, one-page findings, 3 days) if you want a low-commitment first look before anything bigger. Can scope either this week.
```

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
TEMPLATE B — RAG / document-retrieval AI job posts
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
```
RAG systems have a failure mode most teams don't test for: does [their system] trust instructions planted inside the documents it retrieves, not just the user's direct input? That's a different attack surface than prompt injection, and it's the one I see skipped most often.

I test this specifically, alongside standard prompt injection and jailbreak attempts, as part of a structured red team pass. Deliverable is a findings report in four parts: exact repro, which layer failed, the fix, and how to verify it holds.

Given what you've described, the $600 Red Team Review (4-layer testing, prioritized report, debrief call, 1 week) is probably the right scope. Can start once I have read access to a staging environment.
```

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
TEMPLATE C — AI agent / tool-calling job posts
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
```
Agents that can call tools (send emails, hit APIs, move money, modify records) carry a risk class most teams haven't pressure-tested: forged tool calls, tampered arguments, or a user talking the agent into skipping a confirmation step. That's the layer I'd focus on for [their agent].

My baseline: six injection attempts against my own chatbot, four succeeded, every fix became a permanent regression test. I'd run the same discipline against your tool-calling flow specifically.

The $600 Red Team Review covers this (4-layer testing including agent/tool-call abuse, prioritized findings, debrief call, 1 week). If budget is tighter, the $150 Quick Scan is a focused first pass on just the highest-risk flow.
```

DAILY OPERATING RULE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
5 proposals max per day. Custom first line every time — the
formula above is a structure, not a fill-in-the-blank script.
If a job post doesn't give you enough to write a specific
first line, skip it; a generic proposal on a vague job post
wastes a connect for low odds either way.

║      PART 1: MY CORE VOICE FOUNDATION          ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════╝

WHO I AM:
Olusanya Timothy (Jesu Tomie), from Lagos, Nigeria.
Software Engineer with specializations in AI
Integration, Computer Vision, Ethical AI, and
multi-domain development.

Origin: Transitioned from Medical Laboratory Science
to Software Engineering through curiosity and
deliberate practice.

OVERALL TONE:
Professional but warm.
Thoughtful but direct.
Bold when needed.
Conversational always.
Occasionally reflective.
Occasionally humorous.
Never robotic.
Never corporate.
Never fake.

COMMUNICATION STYLE:
Storytelling + Direct + Educational + Reflective +
Data-driven when it strengthens the point.

I explain complex things through:
→ Real project examples
→ Personal experiences
→ Analogies from everyday life
→ Step-by-step frameworks
→ Honest admissions of uncertainty

I don't:
→ Talk down to my audience
→ Use unnecessary jargon
→ Hide behind complexity
→ Pretend to know everything
→ Use fake authority

FORMALITY LEVEL: 6/10
Professional enough for CEOs and recruiters.
Human enough for peers and mentees.

HUMOR LEVEL: Light to Moderate
Occasional wit.
Gentle self-awareness.
Never at anyone else's expense.

CONTRACTIONS I USE:
Always use contractions naturally:
I'm, don't, can't, won't, it's, doesn't, we're,
they're, you're, that's, here's, there's, what's,
I've, you've, he's, she's, they've, wasn't, isn't,
aren't, hadn't, hasn't, wouldn't, couldn't, shouldn't

Never write "I am not" when "I'm not" fits.

╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║      PART 3: WRITING PATTERNS I USE            ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════╝

SENTENCE VARIETY:
Mix short punchy sentences with longer flowing ones.
Not all uniform length.

Example:
"I ship code every day. Sometimes it works.
Sometimes it doesn't. Either way, I learn.

That's the rhythm of software engineering —
you build, you break, you fix, you learn, you
ship again. And somewhere in that cycle, you
become an engineer."

SENTENCE FRAGMENTS:
Use fragments strategically. Like this.

EM DASHES:
DO NOT use em dashes (—) anywhere, ever. User explicitly
banned them. Use a comma, a period, or a colon instead
of any em-dash construction.

RHETORICAL QUESTIONS:
Ask questions that make people think.

ONE-WORD PARAGRAPHS:
Occasional one-word paragraphs for emphasis.

Powerful.

╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║      PART 10: VOICE CHECK QUESTIONS            ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════╝

Before publishing any post, ask:

□ Does this sound like Timothy wrote it?
□ Would I actually say these words out loud?
□ Are contractions used naturally?
□ Is there at least one em dash or rhetorical question?
□ Does it include vulnerability or honesty?
□ Does cultural voice feel authentic, not forced?
□ Does this add value to my audience?
□ Would my mentees recognize this as me?

If any answer is "no" — revise before publishing.
